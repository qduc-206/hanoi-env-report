1. Introduction

The Hanoi Old Quarter (HOQ) is a unique urban heritage site, preserving distinctive historical and cultural values. However, the HOQ faces immense pressure from urban development, leading to a serious degradation of living environment quality and landscape. These issues range from solid waste pollution, declining urban aesthetics, to air pollution and overloaded technical infrastructure. The purpose of this essay is to analyze the evolution and core environmental themes in the HOQ from 2007 to 2025 by synthesizing and evaluating six relevant research papers and news reports, thereby offering a personal assessment and proposing solutions.

2. Methodology

This essay employs a Systematic Literature Review method based on six sources of information collected and arranged chronologically: 2007, 2019, 2020, 2023, 2024, and 2025. These sources include international academic studies (MDPI, 2019, ResearchGate / Longvietarch, 2025), specialized reports (BMKTCN, 2007), and news articles reflecting urgent environmental realities (Kinh Tế Đô Thị, 2023, Môi trường & Cuộc sống, 2020, VOV Giao thông & Dân Việt, 2024). The data is compiled and categorized by key themes: Landscape/Urbanization, Waste Management, and Air Pollution/Policy.

3. Literature Review

The reviewed literature provides a comprehensive view of the shifting focus on environmental issues in the HOQ over time.

3.1. Landscape and Urbanization Focus (2007–2019)

The early focus was on the declining attractiveness of the urban landscape due to development pressure and population growth. The issue later expanded to broader environmental impacts like the Urban Heat Island (UHI) effect (MDPI, 2019).

Source: BMKTCN, 2007

3.2. Waste Crisis and Air Pollution (2020–2023)

This phase reflected urgent public health concerns: overloaded solid waste management after major festivals (Môi trường & Cuộc sống, 2020) and severe air pollution, with the HOQ area recording the highest PM2.5 concentrations in Hoan Kiem District (Kinh Tế Đô Thị, 2023).

3.3. Policy Challenges and Infrastructure Overload (2024–2025)

Pilot programs for source-based waste classification faced difficulties due to cramped living spaces (VOV Giao thông & Dân Việt, 2024). The core issue was confirmed as excessively high population density (823 people/ha) leading to severe infrastructure overload and degraded housing conditions (Longvietarch, 2025).

5. Conclusion,Recommendations And Discussion

5.1. Discussion

The findings reveal that environmental degradation in Hanoi's Old Quater stems from multiple connected factors. Heavy motorbike use causes air, noise, and traffic pollution, while weak waste management leads to garbage buildup that harms the city's image and tourism appeal. Poor enforcement of urban laws and low environmetal awereness among locals an visitors further worseen the situation. Compared with cities like Kyoto or Hoi An, Hanoi lacks strong policies and education programs for a cleaner tourism environment. Thus closer cooperation among authorities, businesses , and the community is vital to improve infrastructure, reduce pollution, and preserve the Old Quater sustainably.

5.2. Conclusion

The literature review demonstrates that environmental issues in the Hanoi Old Quarter are a complex system, evolving from landscape degradation to crises in sanitation, waste, and air pollution, all stemming from the root causes of population overload and overburdened technical infrastructure. A density far exceeding planning limits has paralyzed the effective implementation of waste management solutions and quality of life improvements.

5.3. Recommendations

  • Prioritized Population Relocation (Addressing the Root Cause): Population reduction must be treated as the most urgent environmental solution. Implement attractive financial support policies and build high-quality resettlement areas to reduce density to the planned level (500 people/ha).
  • Environmental Solutions Adapted to Cramped Spaces: For areas where relocation is not feasible, develop smart, high-frequency waste collection and classification models tailored to the cramped tube-house architecture.
  • Promote Low Emission Zones (LEZ) with Green Public Transport: Formally adopt the LEZ (as proposed in 2024) while concurrently strengthening the system of electric/green buses and public bicycles to provide viable alternatives to polluting private vehicles.
